Russia in 2026

A war economy under sanctions, the US-Russia channel after the Dmitriev talks in Washington, and the monetary dimension: ruble, reserves, frozen assets and the digital ruble.

Read the full briefing (PDF) →

18 pages · English · by Aurélie Dupassieux, AD International Consulting

Summary

The briefing at a glance

Russia's war-driven boom has given way to near-stagnation. Growth slowed to 1.0% in 2025 and the central bank expects 0-1% in 2026. Inflation was 6.3% in early September, and the key rate is held at 14%. The budget deficit reached RUB 5.79 tn in January-August, already 50% above the full-year plan, as Ukrainian strikes on refineries and US sanctions on Rosneft and Lukoil squeeze oil revenues. Since 2022 many official statistics have been restricted, so figures should be read with caution.

The pivot to Asia works, but on China's terms: 99% of Russia-China trade is settled in rubles and yuan, and the Power of Siberia 2 pipeline is stuck on price. Diplomacy with Washington restarted in September after a six-month pause. Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met Vladimir Putin in Moscow, and Kirill Dmitriev held talks in Washington on 28-29 September. An energy ceasefire, a Black Sea grain corridor and trilateral talks are on the table. Territory, security guarantees, sanctions and about $300 bn of frozen reserves still block a settlement.

Russia's de-dollarisation is forced rather than chosen. Trade runs through rubles, yuan, the SPFS messaging system and, increasingly, crypto platforms. The central bank sold 43.5 t of gold in the first half of 2026 to cover the deficit, and the largest banks have offered the digital ruble to the public since 1 September.

What it means for businesses

Sanctions first
US and EU rules apply, including secondary sanctions on third-country banks and the EU's 21st package of July 2026.
Payments
Expect settlement mostly in rubles and yuan, with delays, higher fees and conversion risk.
Peace track
Any sanctions relief would be gradual and conditional on a Ukraine settlement, Congress and the EU. Frozen-asset litigation (Euroclear) adds legal risk for some counterparties.
Calendar
Bank of Russia meeting on 23 October; possible trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi.

Key takeaways

What to remember

An economy near stagnation

Growth of 0-1% in 2026, inflation above 6%, a 14% key rate and a deficit well beyond plan.

A pivot east with limits

Asia buys Russian energy, but China sets the terms, as Power of Siberia 2 shows.

Diplomacy restarted, no settlement

The Dmitriev talks keep the US channel open; territory, guarantees and sanctions remain blocked.

Forced de-dollarisation

Rubles and yuan, SPFS, gold sales and the digital ruble: a system built around exclusion.
