Inside the briefing

Five parts, eighteen pages

  1. 01

    Economy and society

    From a war-driven boom to near-stagnation: inflation at 6.3%, a deficit already 50% above the full-year plan, refinery strikes, and a pivot to Asia on China's terms.

  2. 02

    War, peace track and the US

    From the Alaska summit to renewed talks in September 2026, including Kirill Dmitriev's meetings with US Treasury and Energy officials; territory, security guarantees, sanctions and frozen assets remain blocked.

  3. 03

    The case for a US-Russia deal

    What each side would gain, and why any opening depends on a Ukraine settlement, Congress and the EU.

  4. 04

    The monetary dimension

    De-dollarisation by necessity: 99% of Russia-China trade in rubles and yuan, SPFS, gold sales to cover the deficit, and the mass rollout of the digital ruble since 1 September 2026.

  5. 05

    Areas for further analysis

    Domestic politics, the war economy, regions, the Arctic, China, the Global South, Europe, technology and data reliability.

Key takeaways

What to remember

An economy near stagnation

Growth of 0-1% in 2026, inflation above 6%, a 14% key rate and a deficit well beyond plan.

A pivot east with limits

Asia buys Russian energy, but China sets the terms, as Power of Siberia 2 shows.

Diplomacy restarted, no settlement

The Dmitriev talks keep the US channel open; territory, guarantees and sanctions remain blocked.

Forced de-dollarisation

Rubles and yuan, SPFS, gold sales and the digital ruble: a system built around exclusion.
