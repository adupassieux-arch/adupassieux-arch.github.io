France in 2026

Near-stagnation, a sovereign debt test at a 5% yield, relations with Washington after the Évian G7, and the monetary picture: the ECB, the euro, gold and the digital euro.

Read the full briefing (PDF) →

18 pages · English · by Aurélie Dupassieux, AD International Consulting

Summary

The briefing at a glance

French GDP grew 0.9% in 2025, and the Banque de France expects only 0.4% in 2026 after a fall in the first quarter and a flat second quarter. The Middle East war pushed energy prices up: CPI inflation reached 3.1% in September (3.4% on the harmonised measure), and unemployment rose to 8.3%, its highest since 2020. Exports rebounded in the second quarter, led by aerospace, and nuclear power gave France record net electricity exports of 92.3 TWh in 2025.

Public finances are the main test. Debt stands at 119% of GDP and the government expects a deficit of 5.4% in 2026. On 21 October the 10-year OAT yield passed 5% for the first time since 2002, with a spread over the Bund of about 157 bp, the widest since 2011. Moody's kept France at Aa3 with a negative outlook, and the government has no majority in the Assembly to pass the 2027 budget.

With Washington, the relationship is strained but contained. The EU's 15% tariff ceiling has held, while US steel duties at 50% and France's 3% digital services tax remain the flashpoints; France hosted the G7 in Évian in June. The ECB has raised its deposit rate to 2.50% to stop energy prices feeding into wages. The euro holds 20.6% of global reserves, the Banque de France keeps 2,437 tonnes of gold, and a digital euro is not expected before 2029.

What it means for businesses

Funding
Euro funding is dearer after two ECB hikes, and French corporate credit may price off a weaker sovereign.
Tariffs
Most EU goods face a 15% US ceiling; aerospace is exempt and steel stays at 50%.
Currency
EUR/USD near 1.12: hedge dollar revenue and energy costs.
Calendar
ECB on 29 October and 17 December, budget vote by year-end, EU tariff review on 31 December, presidential election in April 2027.

Key takeaways

What to remember

Near-stagnation, inflation back

Growth of about 0.4% in 2026; CPI at 3.1% on energy; unemployment at 8.3%.

A sovereign debt test

Debt at 119% of GDP, a 5% 10-year yield and the widest spread since 2011.

Assets that still hold

Nuclear power and record exports, aerospace, 2,437 t of gold, G7 diplomacy.

Contained transatlantic friction

The 15% ceiling holds; steel and the digital tax are the flashpoints.
