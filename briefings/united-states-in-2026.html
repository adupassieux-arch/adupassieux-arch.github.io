United States in 2026

Growth under an oil shock, trade policy after the Supreme Court ruling, the Fed under a new chair, and the future of the dollar: reserves, gold and stablecoins.

Read the full briefing (PDF) →

18 pages · English · by Aurélie Dupassieux, AD International Consulting

Summary

The briefing at a glance

The US economy grew 2.1% in 2025, and the Federal Reserve projects 2.3% for 2026, driven by consumers and a massive AI investment cycle (about $794 bn of hyperscaler capex this year). The Iran war and disruption in the Strait of Hormuz have kept Brent above $100 and pushed CPI inflation to 3.4% in August; unemployment is 4.1%. Federal debt passed $40 tn on 19 August, the FY2026 deficit is projected at $2.1 tn, and the 10-year Treasury yield stands at 5.26%.

Trade policy has been reshaped by the courts. In February the Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs, leading to about $100 bn of refunds. A temporary 10% surcharge was followed in July by Section 301 tariffs of 10-12.5% on 60 economies, which now face litigation of their own. Diplomacy runs through summits and envoys: Xi Jinping's state visit in September produced a $30 bn tariff deal, and talks with Russia's envoy continued. Record oil output, expected at 13.8 mb/d, cushions the energy shock and lifts US producers, while partners negotiate tariffs case by case.

On 16 September the Fed, chaired by Kevin Warsh since May, raised rates for the first time after five holds, to 3.75-4.00%, citing oil and inflation. Higher US rates support the dollar and tighten financing conditions worldwide. The dollar still accounts for 57% of global FX reserves. Washington rejects a digital dollar and backs private stablecoins instead: the market passed $320 bn in April, and the GENIUS Act takes full effect by January 2027.

What it means for businesses

Funding
Dollar financing is dearer, and futures price a further Fed hike as likely in October.
Tariffs
Check exposure to Section 301 duties, and whether you can claim IEEPA refunds.
Volatility
Oil above $100 and the elections add risk: hedge FX and energy exposure.
Calendar
Midterms on 3 November, APEC in November, federal funding deadline on 11 December.
