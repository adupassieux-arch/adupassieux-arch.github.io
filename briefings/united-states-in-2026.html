Inside the briefing

Five parts, eighteen pages

  1. 01

    Economy and society

    Solid growth driven by consumers and an AI investment cycle worth about 1.8% of GDP, while the Iran war and the Strait of Hormuz push energy prices and headline inflation up. Debt has reached $40 tn and 10-year yields exceed 5%.

  2. 02

    America and the world

    The Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs in February 2026; after a temporary Section 122 surcharge, new Section 301 duties of 10-12.5% apply to 60 economies. September brought Xi Jinping's state visit and the Dmitriev talks.

  3. 03

    The case for engaging Washington

    What allies, rivals and investors gain from dealing with the US, and why deals remain transactional and reversible.

  4. 04

    The monetary dimension

    The Fed's first hike after five holds, the dollar's 57% share of reserves, gold near $4,200 and a $320 bn stablecoin market under the GENIUS Act, with no US central bank digital currency.

  5. 05

    Areas for further analysis

    Midterms, Fed independence, debt dynamics, trade law, the Middle East, the AI boom, China, alliances and labour supply.

Key takeaways

What to remember

Resilient growth, sticky inflation

AI investment and consumers keep growth near 2%; oil keeps inflation above 3%.

Trade policy reset by the courts

IEEPA tariffs struck down and refunded; Section 301 duties now carry the strategy.

Energy power in an oil shock

Record output cushions the US, but Hormuz keeps Brent above $100.

The dollar still dominates

57% of reserves and a rising Fed rate, while debt reaches $40 tn.
