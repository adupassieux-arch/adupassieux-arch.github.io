Inside the briefing

Five parts, eighteen pages

  1. 01

    China's progress

    Growth held at 5%, a 15th Five-Year Plan focused on consumption and innovation, leadership in AI, EVs and renewables, and persistent weaknesses in chips, demographics and property.

  2. 02

    US-China relations

    From tariffs of 145% and 125% at the 2025 peak to the Washington summit of September 2026 and its $30 bn reciprocal tariff cuts; Taiwan, Iran and technology remain fault lines.

  3. 03

    The case for partnership

    What the US, China and the world gain from stability, within a transactional, de-risking relationship.

  4. 04

    The monetary dimension

    The yuan's rise has stalled at 2.75% of SWIFT payments; China buys gold, holds fewer Treasuries and scales up the interest-bearing e-CNY and the mBridge platform.

  5. 05

    Areas for further analysis

    Political system, human rights, regional security, China and the world, Europe, economic fragilities, resources, capital account and data reliability.

Key takeaways

What to remember

Technological and industrial power

Leads the energy transition in installed capacity; at the forefront of AI, EVs and batteries.

Structural fragilities

Weak domestic demand, demographics, local debt, property, grid integration of renewables.

US-China relations

Truce extended and a $30 bn deal, but Taiwan, Iran and technology remain unresolved.

Monetary strategy

Hedging against the dollar through gold, CIPS and the e-CNY, without yet challenging it.
