China in 2026

Progress, vulnerabilities, relations with the United States after the Washington summit, and the monetary dimension: yuan internationalisation, de-dollarisation and the e-CNY.

Read the full briefing (PDF) →

18 pages · English · by Aurélie Dupassieux, AD International Consulting

Summary

The briefing at a glance

China grew 5.0% in 2025, meeting its official target, with GDP close to ¥140 tn (about $19.7 tn). The 15th Five-Year Plan (2026-30) aims to rebalance the economy towards household consumption, innovation and the low-carbon transition. China now ranks 10th in the Global Innovation Index (34th in 2012) and leads in AI, electric vehicles, batteries and renewables: wind and solar capacity reached about 2,000 GW by mid-2026, and solar overtook coal in July. The weak points are semiconductors, a shrinking and ageing population, local debt, the property crisis, and a grid that curtailed over a quarter of wind and solar output in the first half of 2026.

Relations with Washington have moved from escalation, with tariffs of 145% and 125% in April 2025, to a partial thaw. During Xi Jinping's state visit in September 2026, the two sides set up a Board of Trade to cut tariffs on about $30 bn of goods each way and extended their truce to 10 January 2027. Since the Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs, US duties rest on Sections 301 and 232, and no date has been set for the cuts. Taiwan, Iran and technology remain outside any compromise.

On the monetary side, Beijing hedges against the dollar rather than challenging it. The yuan settles 28% of China's goods trade, but its share of SWIFT payments has slipped to 2.75%. US Treasury holdings are down to $618 bn, the lowest since 2008; gold reserves have risen for 22 consecutive months to 2,387 t; and the e-CNY, interest-bearing since January 2026, is moving into cross-border payments through mBridge.

What it means for businesses

Trade
The $30 bn package mainly covers consumer and farm goods. Check whether your products are listed, and plan for Section 301 and 232 duties until the cuts take effect.
Currency
Yuan invoicing can lower conversion costs on China-facing trade. Hedge CNY/CNH exposure: the Fed's hike to 3.75-4.00% widens the rate gap, and capital controls remain.
Compliance
CIPS and mBridge offer routes outside the dollar system, but Western authorities watch these channels closely.
Calendar
APEC summit in Shenzhen in November; truce deadline on 10 January 2027.

Inside the briefing

Five parts, eighteen pages

  1. 01

    China's progress

    Growth held at 5%, a 15th Five-Year Plan focused on consumption and innovation, leadership in AI, EVs and renewables, and persistent weaknesses in chips, demographics and property.

  2. 02

    US-China relations

    From tariffs of 145% and 125% at the 2025 peak to the Washington summit of September 2026, its $30 bn reciprocal tariff cuts and a truce extended to January 2027; the legal basis of US tariffs after the Supreme Court ruling; Taiwan, Iran and technology remain fault lines.

  3. 03

    The case for partnership

    What the US, China and the world gain from stability, within a transactional, de-risking relationship.

  4. 04

    The monetary dimension

    The yuan's rise has stalled at 2.75% of SWIFT payments; China buys gold, holds fewer Treasuries and scales up the interest-bearing e-CNY and the mBridge platform.

  5. 05

    Areas for further analysis

    Political system, human rights, regional security, China and the world, Europe, economic fragilities, resources, capital account and data reliability.

Key takeaways

What to remember

Technological and industrial power

Leads the energy transition in installed capacity; at the forefront of AI, EVs and batteries.

Structural fragilities

Weak domestic demand, demographics, local debt, property, grid integration of renewables.

US-China relations

Truce extended to January 2027 and a $30 bn deal, but Taiwan, Iran and technology remain unresolved.

Monetary strategy

Hedging against the dollar through gold, CIPS and the e-CNY, without yet challenging it.
