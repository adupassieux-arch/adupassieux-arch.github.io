Light Cycle Oil in Asia: Omani Supply in 2026

A market note on light cycle oil: what the product is, why Omani supply loaded outside the Strait of Hormuz matters in 2026, how cargoes are priced and traded, and what buyers should watch.

10 pages · English · by Aurélie Dupassieux, AD International Consulting

Summary

The note at a glance

Light cycle oil (LCO) is a middle distillate made in the fluid catalytic cracking units of refineries. Rich in aromatics, it is used for diesel and gasoil blending, as a feedstock for carbon black, in marine fuel blends and for aromatics extraction. Its value depends on sulphur and aromatics content, and it is usually priced against Platts Singapore gasoil or fuel oil plus or minus a differential.

Oman has gained relevance as an origin in 2026. Its export refineries at Sohar, on the Gulf of Oman, and Duqm, on the Arabian Sea, load outside the Strait of Hormuz, which was closed from mid-March to April and disrupted again in September. The country holds investment-grade ratings from S&P and Fitch and is not subject to sanctions. Other bypass routes exist through Fujairah and Yanbu, but the Saudi East-West pipeline was hit in September.

Cargo values are large: a 50,000-tonne cargo holds about 337,000 barrels, worth roughly $17-31 million at LCO prices of $52-93 a barrel. Trade typically runs FOB the load port or CFR/CIF Asia, against a documentary letter of credit at sight, with independent inspection and English law.

What it means for buyers

Diversify origins
Supply loaded outside the Strait of Hormuz reduces exposure to Gulf disruptions.
Verify quality
Ask for a full spec sheet, a pre-shipment sample and load-port inspection.
Secure payment
Use a documentary L/C at sight; avoid advance payments.
Screen counterparties
KYC, OFAC/EU/UN sanctions lists and certificates of origin.
